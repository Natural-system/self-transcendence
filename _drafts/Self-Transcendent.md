### 1. Introduction

Within the classical theoretical lineage of personality psychology, most models are built upon "norms" and "normal distributions," with the underlying assumption that human psychological activity follows a "deficit-compensation" cycle. Whether it is the individual torn between instinct and superego in Freud's framework, or the striver pursuing superiority out of inferiority in Adler's view, neither adequately encompasses certain extreme, integrative psychological-ecological specimens. In his later years, Maslow recognized that above self-actualization there exists a state of "self-transcendence"—wherein the individual expands their focus from self-actualization to a broader concern for the cosmos, life in all its forms, and humanity as a whole. However, due to the scarcity of such specimens, relevant descriptions remain largely theoretical extrapolations, lacking detailed case-based empirical support.

This paper aims to present a living case of a possible "self-transcendent personality" through a comprehensive self-analysis report. By deeply reconstructing his own behavioral patterns, cognitive architecture, emotional mechanisms, and life trajectory, the author demonstrates how a "highly integrated" and "anti-fragile" psychological system is formed. This report is not merely a personal personality inventory, but aspires to offer a reference for psychology, anthropology, and sociology regarding human potential and consciousness development.
